U-KAST: Kolmogorov-Arnold Swin Transformer for Medical Image Segmentation

Nishchal Sapkota, Haoyan Shi, Yejia Zhang, Xianshi Ma, Bofang Zheng, Fabian Vazquez, Pengfei Gu , Danny Z. Chen
ISBI • 2026
Abstract
Medical image segmentation is critical for accurate diagnostics and treatment planning, but remains challenging due to complex anatomical structures and limited annotated training data. We introduce UKAST, a U-Net-like architecture that integrates rational-function-based Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) into Swin Transformer encoders. By leveraging rational base functions and Group Rational KANs, UKAST improves on the inefficiencies of vanilla spline-based KANs, yielding a more expressive and data-efficient framework with reduced FLOPs and only a very small increase in parameter count compared to SwinUNETR. UKAST achieves state-of-the-art performance on four diverse 2D and 3D medical image segmentation benchmarks, consistently surpassing CNN- and Transformer-based baselines, including in data-scarce settings.

D-GEM: Sparsely Supervised Surgical Video Segmentation

Nishchal Sapkota, Yejia Zhang, Bofang Zheng, Xianshi Ma, Haoyan Shi, Yohannes Mariam, Danny Z. Chen
MICCAI • 2026
Abstract
Surgical video segmentation often operates on long image sequences for which dense annotation is prohibitively expensive, making sparse supervision more practical. We propose a reliability-gated asymmetric dual-memory framework built on self-supervised visual representations of DINOv3. Our model accounts for short-term temporal continuity and long-term semantic stability using a gated transient memory with bounded capacity and an evolving anchor memory that incrementally builds semantic representations without requiring complete first-frame class coverage. By decoupling memory from the encoder, the framework enables direct use of pre-trained vision foundation encoders and facilitates data-efficient adaptation under surgical-video domain shift. Experiments on multiple surgical video datasets demonstrate improved temporal consistency and robustness compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
